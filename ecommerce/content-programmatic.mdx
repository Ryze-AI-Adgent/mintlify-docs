Programmatic content is generating pages at scale from a structured data source, each targeting distinct long-tail demand. Done well it reaches queries too specific to write for individually. Done badly it produces thin content that damages the whole site.

## How Ryze generates content

Topics come from the [keyword research](/ecommerce/keyword-research) pipeline — real queries with real volume and difficulty, anchored to your catalogue and Search Console data.

Each topic becomes a full article: body, meta, and a featured image, with internal links to your own key pages, published to your store or CMS.

Articles are planned in [two pools](/how-ryze-works#two-content-pools) — priority-anchored and open — so coverage is both focused on what matters and broad enough to catch adjacent demand.

## Pacing

The engine runs on a **monthly cycle tied to your billing date**:

- It works toward a target volume for the cycle
- It produces only the **deficit** — what is still missing — rather than duplicating work already done
- Output is **spread across the month**, not published in a batch

Spreading matters. A thousand pages appearing overnight is a pattern search engines treat with suspicion; steady publication is not.

## Avoiding thin content

The failure mode of programmatic content is thousands of near-identical pages differing by a swapped variable. See [programmatic SEO](/concepts/programmatic-seo).

Guardrails that matter:

- **One page per distinct intent**, never several per intent
- **Real data per page**, not templated prose with variables
- **Skip topics with insufficient source material** rather than generating padding
- **Internal links specific to the page**, not a shared template footer
- **Measure before scaling** — impressions per batch in Search Console
- **Prune** pages that never earn impressions

## Where it fits against product pages

Programmatic articles target informational and long-tail queries. They are not a substitute for good [product](/shopify/product-pages) and [category](/ecommerce/category-pages) pages, which serve commercial intent.

The relationship: articles capture upper-funnel demand and pass authority to commercial pages through internal links. An article that links nowhere useful is wasted.

## Reviewing output

Articles ship under your [approval settings](/claude/permissions). Watch what is published in **Agent Activity**, which logs every draft and publish with a timestamp.

If topics are off-target, fix [agent context](/agent/context) rather than rejecting individual topics — context is what everything is anchored to.

## Expectations

New articles take weeks to months to be crawled, indexed, and ranked. Organic movement typically appears at 2–6 weeks, and compounds after that. Week one produces published work, not results.
