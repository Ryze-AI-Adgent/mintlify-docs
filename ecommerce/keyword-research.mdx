Keyword research for ecommerce starts from what you already sell and already rank for, not from a blank keyword tool.

## How Ryze does it

<Steps>
  <Step title="Establish ground truth">
    Pull Google Search Console (queries and pages you already receive impressions for), DataForSEO (rankings, top pages, competitors), and your store (best products by revenue).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Derive seeds">
    Keyword seeds are derived from what you actually sell and who you sell to — anchored to the catalogue rather than guessed.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Expand">
    Seeds expand through DataForSEO into real keyword ideas with actual search volume and difficulty attached.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select">
    Candidates are narrowed to those worth chasing given your current authority and existing coverage.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Ryze never invents metrics. Volume and difficulty come from real DataForSEO data.

## Selection criteria

**Intent match.** A keyword must match a page type you can actually serve. Informational queries want articles; commercial queries want category pages. Targeting a commercial query with a blog post wastes the effort.

**Achievable difficulty.** Difficulty is relative to your domain authority. A term the market leader owns is not a target for a new store regardless of volume.

**Existing traction.** Queries where you already receive impressions but rank poorly are the highest-ROI targets — demand is proven and the page exists. This is where Ryze looks first.

**Distinct intent.** Ten keyword variants meaning the same thing get one page, not ten. Multiple pages for one intent compete with each other.

## Where ecommerce keyword research goes wrong

- **Chasing head terms.** "boots" has volume and is unwinnable. "wide fit waterproof walking boots" converts better and is reachable.
- **Ignoring existing impressions.** Search Console already tells you what you nearly rank for. Most stores never look.
- **One page per variant.** Produces internal competition, not coverage.
- **Volume without intent.** High-volume informational terms bring traffic that does not buy.
- **Ignoring the long tail.** Most catalogue revenue sits in queries too specific to target individually — which is what [programmatic content](/ecommerce/content-programmatic) is for.

## Two pools

Ryze plans topics in two pools so coverage is both focused and broad:

- **Pool A — priority-anchored.** Built around priority keywords and highest-value products.
- **Pool B — open.** Expands into adjacent demand from Search Console impressions, competitor gaps, and the wider catalogue.

See [how Ryze works](/how-ryze-works).

## Reviewing the output

If topics look off-target, the cause is almost always [agent context](/agent/context) — what you sell, who you sell to. That is the input everything downstream is anchored to. Fix it there rather than rejecting topics one at a time.
