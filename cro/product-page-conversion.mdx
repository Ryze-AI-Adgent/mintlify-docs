The product page is where the buying decision happens. Most product pages fail not because they look wrong but because they leave a question unanswered, and an unanswered question is an abandoned cart.

## Answer the buying questions

A buyer needs to know, before deciding:

- **Will it fit / work for me?** Complete specifications, sizing, compatibility
- **What does it actually look like?** Multiple images, zoom, scale reference, video where useful
- **What will it cost me in total?** Shipping cost shown before checkout, not after
- **When will it arrive?** Delivery estimate on the page
- **What if it's wrong?** Returns window and cost, stated plainly
- **Can I trust this?** Reviews, contact details, policy visibility

Every one of these that is missing or hidden is a reason to leave and check a competitor.

## Shipping cost is the biggest single lever

Unexpected shipping cost revealed at checkout is consistently among the largest causes of cart abandonment. Showing it on the product page loses some clicks and gains completed purchases.

The same applies to delivery time. "Delivered in 2–4 days" converts better than silence, even when the answer is not fast.

## Specifications sell

Complete, scannable specifications do double duty: they help buyers decide, and they make the page extractable by AI systems and shopping agents. See [making product pages AI-readable](/ai-visibility/ai-readable-pages).

State attributes as data — material, dimensions, weight, fit, compatibility — not implied in prose. Never put specifications only in an image.

## Design for mobile first

Most ecommerce traffic is mobile, and most product pages are designed on a desktop.

- Price and add-to-cart visible without scrolling
- Tap targets large enough for variant pickers
- Images swipeable, zoomable
- Specifications reachable without hunting through tabs
- No layout shift that causes mis-taps

## Reviews

Reviews reduce uncertainty more than any copy you write. Show them, including imperfect ones — a product with only five-star reviews reads as filtered and lowers trust.

Mark them up with valid structured data, but only where they genuinely exist and are visible. See [product structured data](/shopify/product-schema).

## Speed

A slow product page loses buyers before any of the above matters. Imagery is usually the cause. See [site speed](/shopify/site-speed).

## Test rather than assume

Product page changes are testable and worth testing on high-traffic templates. See [A/B testing](/cro/ab-testing).
