Everything Ryze writes — programmatic articles, on-page copy, off-page mentions, meta titles — is shaped by one place: your **Brand** context. It's the brief the agents read on every turn. Get it right and every draft sounds like you, sells what you actually sell, and points at the pages and sources you care about.

Find it under **Brand** in the workspace sidebar. It's split into three tabs: **Identity**, **Visual**, and **Context**.

## Identity — what you sell and how you sound

This is the foundation every agent starts from.

- **What you sell.** A short description of your offer, plus your **industry**. This anchors topic planning, keyword relevance, and the angle of every article — so a deceased-loved-one persona platform never gets written up like a generic SaaS.
- **Tone of voice.** How the writing should feel. Be specific and prescriptive — the agent follows this literally. For example: *"Writes with quiet emotional directness — short sentences, plain language, a willingness to sit with hard feelings rather than paper over them. Avoids corporate enthusiasm, feature-list marketing, and hollow superlatives. Favours honest qualifications. It's not a real person, it's gentle candour."*

The more concrete the tone, the less generic the output. Vague tone ("professional, friendly") gives you vague, average copy. Sharp tone gives you copy that sounds like nobody else.

## Context — steer the substance and the links

The **Context** tab is where you go beyond voice and steer *what the agent knows and references*.

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/seo-agent-context-notes.webp" alt="The Brand page Context tab — what the agent should always remember, writing style preferences, and blog examples" />
</Frame>

### What the agent should always remember

Free-form notes the agent reads before writing anything. This is your lever for facts, claims, and guardrails that should show up across all content:

- Hard facts about your product, pricing, and positioning the AI must get right.
- Claims you *can* make and ones you **must not** (legal, medical, compliance).
- Differentiators and proof points you want surfaced.
- Competitors or comparisons to avoid.

### Writing style preferences

Finer-grained rules than tone of voice — the mechanics of how a sentence is built. Things like: short paragraphs, no em dashes, British spelling, never start with "In today's world", always use active voice. The agent applies these to every draft.

### Influencing external references (pSEO)

Programmatic articles and off-page mentions naturally cite and link out to sources. You steer that here:

- **Name the sources you want cited** — industry reports, your own research, authoritative sites in your space — in the "always remember" notes. The agent pulls them in when relevant.
- **Name what to avoid** — competitors you don't want to send link equity to, low-quality sources, anything off-brand.
- **Point at your own pages.** Programmatic articles anchor internal links to your own key pages — drawn automatically from your store and Search Console. Context shapes the *external* references the agent cites; your real pages shape the *internal* ones. Together they decide where every article points.

### Blog examples

Paste a few URLs or excerpts of writing you want the agent to emulate — your best existing posts, or a competitor's tone you admire. The agent uses them as a stylistic reference, matching structure and rhythm rather than copying.

## Why it matters

The agents run on this context every single turn — there's no separate "settings" they forget. Edit the Brand page once and the change shows up in the next article, the next optimization, the next mention. It's the highest-leverage place in Ryze: a few minutes here changes everything downstream.

<Note>
Brand context is per-workspace. If you run [multiple workspaces](/workspaces/create-workspace), each one carries its own identity, tone, and notes — so a single account can serve very different brands without bleed-over.
</Note>
