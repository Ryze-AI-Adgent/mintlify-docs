title: "Switching from Surfer"
description: "How Surfer and Ryze differ on content optimization, what changes when you move from assisted writing to automated publishing."
---

Surfer optimises content you are writing. Ryze writes and publishes content for you. The difference is where the human sits in the process.

## What each does

| | Surfer | Ryze |
|---|---|---|
| Content optimization scoring | Yes | Not as a score |
| SERP-based term recommendations | Yes | Built into generation |
| **Writes full articles** | Assisted | Yes, automated |
| **Publishes to your site** | Limited | Yes, direct to store or CMS |
| Keyword research | Yes | Yes, via DataForSEO |
| Technical SEO fixes | No | Yes |
| Backlink building | No | Yes |
| Paid ads | No | Yes |

## The workflow difference

**Surfer** assumes a writer. You choose a topic, Surfer analyses ranking pages, and you write against a term list and content score.

**Ryze** runs the whole loop: it selects topics from your Search Console and catalogue data, generates the article with internal links, and publishes it — on a monthly cycle, paced across the month, under your approval settings.

If you have a content team who write, Surfer supports them. If you do not, Ryze replaces the need for one.

## On content scores

Surfer's optimization score is useful as a guide and unreliable as a target. Writing to maximise a score produces text stuffed with terms because a competitor used them, which is not the same as writing something worth citing.

Ryze does not produce a score. Content is generated from real demand data and your catalogue, with structure aimed at being extractable and useful. See [programmatic content](/ecommerce/content-programmatic).

## What does not transfer

- **Content briefs and outlines** stay in Surfer
- **Optimization history and scores** do not carry over
- **Saved keyword and topic lists** have no import path

Published articles are unaffected — they live on your site and remain yours.

## Before you cancel

1. Export any **briefs or outlines** you plan to write
2. Export **curated keyword and topic lists**
3. Note **articles in progress**, so they are not lost mid-workflow

## Setting expectations after switching

The main adjustment is editorial control. Surfer gives you per-article control; Ryze operates at cycle level.

You control it through:

- **[Agent context](/agent/context)** — what you sell, who you sell to, tone. This is the main quality lever, and the most common cause of off-target output.
- **[Approvals](/claude/permissions)** — require sign-off before publishing if you want a review step
- **Agent Activity** — every draft and publish, timestamped

If topics look wrong, fix agent context rather than rejecting topics individually.
