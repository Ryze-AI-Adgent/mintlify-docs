Surfer helps you write and score content, and its Surfer AI can draft full articles one at a time. Ryze plans, writes and publishes content for you, on a schedule. The difference is where the human sits in the process.

## What each does

| | Surfer | Ryze |
|---|---|---|
| Content optimization scoring | Yes | Yes — **Article score** (0–100) from nine checks |
| SERP-based term recommendations | Yes | Built into generation |
| **Writes full articles** | Yes (Surfer AI, per article) | Yes, automated and scheduled |
| **Publishes to your site** | Limited | Yes, direct to store or CMS |
| Keyword research | Yes | Yes, via DataForSEO |
| Technical SEO fixes | No | Yes |
| Backlink building | No | Yes |
| Paid ads | No | Yes |

## The workflow difference

**Surfer** assumes a writer. You choose a topic, Surfer analyses ranking pages, and you write against a term list and content score.

**Ryze** runs the whole loop: it selects topics from your Search Console and catalogue data, generates the article with internal links, and publishes it — up to 3 articles a day on weekdays, under your publishing settings.

If you have a content team who write, Surfer supports them. If you do not, Ryze replaces the need for one.

## On content scores

Surfer's optimization score is useful as a guide and unreliable as a target. Writing to maximise a score produces text stuffed with terms because a competitor used them, which is not the same as writing something worth citing.

Every article in the Content Plan gets an **Article score** out of 100 from nine checks — content structure, internal and external links, statistics, image alt text, semantic keywords, an FAQ section, meta tags and JSON-LD schema. It's a checklist of what the article covers, not a target to stuff terms into. Topics come from real demand data and your catalogue. See [Content Plan](/seo/programmatic).

## What does not transfer

- **Content briefs and outlines** stay in Surfer
- **Optimization history and scores** do not carry over
- **Saved keyword and topic lists** have no import path

Published articles are unaffected — they live on your site and remain yours.

## Before you cancel

1. Export any **briefs or outlines** you plan to write
2. Export **curated keyword and topic lists**
3. Note **articles in progress**, so they are not lost mid-workflow

## Setting expectations after switching

The main adjustment is editorial control. In Surfer you control each article as you write it; in Ryze the agent plans and writes the articles, and you steer the inputs and review drafts.

You control it through:

- **[Brand](/agent/context)** (sidebar → **Brand**) — what you sell, who you sell to, tone. This is the main quality lever, and the most common cause of off-target output.
- **Publishing mode** — set **SEO → Preferences → Publishing → Publishing mode** to **Manual** if every article should wait for your approval before it goes live. See [SEO Preferences](/seo/preferences).
- **Edit** — open any draft in **SEO → Content Plan** and change the title, meta tags, body or scheduled date before it publishes.

If topics look wrong, fix your Brand profile rather than rejecting topics individually.
