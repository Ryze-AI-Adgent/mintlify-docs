Everything Ryze produces — programmatic articles, on-page copy, off-page mentions, ad creatives, reports — is shaped by one place: your **Brand** profile. It's the brief the agents read on every turn. Get it right and every output sounds like you, sells what you actually sell, and follows your rules.

Find it under **Brand** in the sidebar. Ryze builds the first version by reading your site; after that you refine it by hand, or click **Improve Brand Profile** and the agent reviews what's there, flags anything weak or missing, and asks before changing anything.

At the top is your brand description — what the company does, for whom, and what makes it different. Below it, four tabs: **Identity**, **Visual**, **Context**, and **Memory**.

## Identity — what you sell and how you sound

- **What you sell.** Your **offer**, plus your **industry**. This anchors topic planning, keyword relevance, and the angle of every piece — so a deceased-loved-one persona platform never gets written up like a generic SaaS.
- **Tone of voice.** How the brand should sound. Be specific and prescriptive — the agent follows this literally. For example: *"Writes with quiet emotional directness — short sentences, plain language, a willingness to sit with hard feelings rather than paper over them. Avoids corporate enthusiasm, feature-list marketing, and hollow superlatives."*

The more concrete the tone, the less generic the output. Vague tone ("professional, friendly") gives you vague, average copy.

## Visual — colors and fonts

Your brand **colors** and **fonts**, which the agent applies to every ad creative it generates.

Logos, product photos and videos don't live here — upload them to **Media** in the sidebar, and the agent picks from them before it looks anywhere else. See [Media library](/workspaces/media) and [AI Ad Creatives](/paid-ads/ai-creatives).

## Context — what the agent should always remember

Free-form notes the agent reads before doing anything, in chat and in background work. This is your lever for facts, claims, and guardrails:

- Hard facts about your product, pricing, and positioning the AI must get right.
- Claims you *can* make and ones you **must not** (legal, medical, compliance).
- Differentiators and proof points you want surfaced.
- Competitors or comparisons to avoid.
- Sources you want cited — industry reports, your own research, authoritative sites in your space — and sources to avoid.

## Memory — what the agent has learned

Short facts and rules about your business, one per memory: "Never set a daily budget above $200 without asking", "campaigns are set up per country". The agent adds memories itself when you tell it something it should keep in mind next time, and you can **Add memory**, **Edit** or **Delete** any of them. Delete a memory and the agent no longer sees it.

## Writing rules for articles

Rules that only apply to SEO articles — tone for articles, custom writing instructions (short paragraphs, no em dashes, British spelling), and article examples the writer mimics for structure and style — live in **SEO → Preferences → Content**. See [SEO Preferences](/seo/preferences).

## Why it matters

The agents run on this context every single turn — there's no separate "settings" they forget. Edit the Brand page once and the change shows up in the next article, the next optimization, the next creative. It's the highest-leverage place in Ryze: a few minutes here changes everything downstream.

<Note>
Brand is per-workspace. If you run [multiple workspaces](/workspaces/create-workspace), each one carries its own identity, tone, notes and memories — so a single account can serve very different brands without bleed-over.
</Note>
