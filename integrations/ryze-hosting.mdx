Ryze Hosting is a publishing destination for sites without a CMS. Ryze hosts your blog — on a free Ryze subdomain such as `your-brand.byryze.com`, or on a domain you own, like `blog.example.com`. Articles then publish straight to your blog, with HTTPS handled for you.

Your site runs on a platform Ryze doesn't connect to? Host the blog here and link to it from your site — see [Don't see your platform?](/integrations/overview#dont-see-your-platform).

## Step 1 — Choose where your blog lives

On the **Integrations** page, click **Connect** on the **Ryze Hosting** card. Ryze asks you to *Choose where your hosted blog lives* and shows two options:

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/ryze-hosting-choose-domain.png" alt="The Ryze Hosting dialog with two options: Free Ryze subdomain and Your own domain" />
</Frame>

- **Free Ryze subdomain** — `your-brand.byryze.com`. Click it, type a name (letters, numbers and dashes; Ryze suggests one from your organization name) and click **Connect domain**. The blog goes live in about a minute, with no DNS setup. You're done — skip Steps 2 and 3.
- **Your own domain** — click it, enter the domain and click **Connect domain**, then follow Steps 2 and 3:
  - **A subdomain** (recommended) — e.g. `blog.example.com`, keeping your main site untouched.
  - **Your root domain** — e.g. `example.com`, if the whole domain is for the blog.

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/ryze-hosting-custom-domain.png" alt="Entering your own blog domain, with Setup guide and Connect domain" />
</Frame>

You can start on the free subdomain and move to your own domain later: click **View** on the **Ryze Hosting** card, then **Connect custom domain**.

## Step 2 — Add the DNS record

Ryze shows the exact record to add at your domain provider. Which one depends on what you entered:

| Domain type | Record | Name | Value |
|-------------|--------|------|-------|
| Subdomain (`blog.example.com`) | **CNAME** | `blog` | `cname.vercel-dns.com` |
| Root domain (`example.com`) | **A** | `@` | `76.76.21.21` |

If Ryze also shows a **verification** record (a `TXT` or extra `CNAME`), add that one too — it's needed when the domain is already used elsewhere.

Ryze detects your DNS provider — Cloudflare, GoDaddy, Namecheap, Route 53, Vercel, Google Domains, DigitalOcean, Name.com, DNSimple, or Porkbun:

- For some providers Ryze shows a **Set up automatically in …** button with your provider's name (for example, **Set up automatically in Cloudflare**). Click it, sign in to your provider and approve the change — Ryze verifies the record on its own. The record is listed below the button under **Or add this DNS record yourself**, if you'd rather add it by hand.
- Otherwise Ryze shows *Add this DNS record at your domain provider* — click **Open … DNS settings** (for example, **Open GoDaddy DNS settings**) and add the record yourself.

<Frame>

</Frame>

<Warning>
On **Cloudflare**, add the record exactly as Ryze shows it — for a subdomain, a **CNAME** named `blog` pointing to `cname.vercel-dns.com` — and set it to **DNS only** (grey cloud), not proxied (orange cloud). A proxied record breaks verification.
</Warning>

## Step 3 — Verify

Back in Ryze, click **Verify**. Ryze checks your DNS and verifies the domain once the record resolves — usually a few minutes, up to about an hour for DNS to propagate. It keeps re-checking on its own, so you can close the dialog and come back.

Once verified, HTTPS is issued automatically and your blog is live at your domain.

## Change or remove the domain

Click **View** on the **Ryze Hosting** card, then **Remove domain**:

- If the blog was on your own domain, it moves back to a free Ryze subdomain and article links update automatically. You can connect a custom domain again any time.
- If the blog was on the free subdomain, it goes offline immediately — published article links stop working.

## Publishing to it

With Ryze Hosting connected, it works like any other publishing destination — programmatic articles publish to your hosted blog on schedule. See [Programmatic Articles](/seo/programmatic).

## Keeping the hosted blog out of Google

If you publish the same articles to your own CMS as well, the hosted copy can compete with your site for the same searches. You can switch the hosted copy off search entirely: in **Blog Studio → SEO**, under **Keep this blog out of Google**, turn on **Hide this blog from search engines**.

When it's on, three things change together so nothing contradicts: the blog's `robots.txt` disallows crawling, the sitemap lists no articles, and every hosted page (index and articles) carries a `noindex, nofollow` tag. The SEO panel notes that the blog is hidden while the toggle is on. Switch it off to let search engines index the hosted blog again.

<Note>
This only affects the Ryze-hosted copy — articles published to your own CMS are not touched. Hosted blogs on the default `*.byryze.com` address also don't take part in the [Backlink Exchange](/seo/backlink-exchange); a custom domain connected here does.
</Note>


## Troubleshooting

- **Not verifying** — DNS can take up to an hour. Confirm the record type matches your domain (CNAME for a subdomain, A for a root), and that the name and value are exact.
- **Cloudflare stuck** — the record must be **DNS only** (grey cloud). Switch off the proxy and check again.
- **Verification record ignored** — if Ryze showed a second (verification) record, both records must be present.
- **"This subdomain is reserved. Pick another name."** — that name can't be used. Type a different one.
- **"This domain is already connected to another workspace"** — the domain is in use in another Ryze workspace. Remove it there first (**View** → **Remove domain**), or use a different domain.
- **"Use letters, numbers and dashes only"** — the subdomain name has other characters. Remove spaces, dots and symbols.

Still stuck? Email [support@get-ryze.ai](mailto:support@get-ryze.ai).
